What Does a Metallic Epoxy Install Involve?

A metallic epoxy install starts with grinding and patching the concrete until it is smooth and flat. Metallic floors are glossy and reflective, so any dip, crack or patch underneath can show through.

A dark or tinted base coat goes down first. Then the metallic layer is poured, spread and worked with rollers, brushes and sometimes a leaf blower or solvent spray to move the pigment into the pattern you chose.

After it cures, a clear topcoat protects the design. For garages that get sun, a UV-stable topcoat keeps the colors from yellowing.

Why Do Metallic Floors Show Every Flaw in the Slab?

Metallic floors show slab flaws because they are mirror-glossy and the pigment flows toward low spots while it is wet. A dip in the concrete collects more pigment and shows up as a darker patch, and cracks can print through as lines.

That is why prep on a metallic floor goes further than on a flake floor. Cracks are filled and ground flat, high spots are ground down, and sometimes a skim coat is used to even the surface before the color goes down.

Flake floors hide most of these problems with texture. Metallic floors do the opposite, so a careful prep step is what separates a great metallic floor from a blotchy one.

What Affects the Cost of Metallic Epoxy Flooring in Fontana?

The cost of metallic epoxy flooring depends on the size of the room, how flat the slab is, and how complex the design will be. A single-color swirl is simpler to lay out than a multi-color pattern with veining.

Slab leveling and crack repair are the biggest variables, because metallic floors need a smoother base than other coatings. The choice of topcoat and any anti-slip additive also changes the quote.

Metallic vs. Flake: Which Do You Need?

Both are full epoxy systems over ground concrete. They are built for different goals.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a metallic epoxy floor slippery?

A metallic epoxy floor is smooth and glossy, so it can be slippery when wet. A fine clear grit can be added to the topcoat for better grip without hiding the design.

Can I choose the exact pattern of a metallic floor?

You choose the colors and the general style, such as marble, lava or ocean-like swirls. The exact pattern forms as the pigment moves in the wet resin, so every metallic floor is unique.

Does a metallic epoxy floor scratch?

A metallic epoxy floor can show fine scratches like any glossy surface, mostly from dragged grit. A hard topcoat, entry mats and furniture pads keep it looking new for years.

Can metallic epoxy go in a garage?

Metallic epoxy works in a garage, especially one used for display cars or as a gym. Use a UV-stable topcoat if the door is often open, and expect it to show dust more than a flake floor.
