What Does a Garage Floor Epoxy Install Involve?

A garage floor epoxy install starts with grinding. A diamond grinder with a dust vacuum takes off the top skin of the concrete, along with old sealers, paint and tire residue, and leaves a surface that feels like medium sandpaper.

Next, cracks and pits are filled and ground flush. The base coat is rolled on, and colored flakes are thrown across the whole floor until no base color shows. Once it hardens, the loose flakes are scraped and vacuumed off.

The last layer is a clear topcoat, usually polyaspartic for garages that get sun. It seals the flakes, adds gloss and takes the daily wear from tires and boots.

When Do You Need Garage Floor Epoxy?

Peeling or flaking coating

If an earlier coating is lifting in sheets, it was likely never bonded. It has to be ground off before a new floor goes down.

Stains that keep coming back

Oil and coolant soak deep into bare concrete. Coating the slab seals it so the next spill wipes off with a rag.

Constant dust

Uncoated concrete slowly powders on its surface. A sealed floor ends the gray dust that settles on everything you store.

Hot tire marks

Dull rings under the car mean the current coating is too thin or too soft for summer driving.

New gym or workshop

A garage that doubles as a gym or shop needs a floor that handles drops, rolling carts and spills.

Fresh look before selling

A finished garage floor helps the house show well to buyers walking through it.

Why Does Garage Floor Epoxy Peel?

Garage floor epoxy peels when it never bonded to the concrete in the first place. The most common cause is skipping the grinding step and using an acid wash instead. Acid does not remove sealers or the tire chemicals baked into the slab, so the coating sits on top and lets go under stress.

Moisture is the second cause. Slabs poured without a vapor barrier can push water vapor up from the ground, and that pressure lifts the coating from below in blisters. Older homes are the usual suspects, which is why we test before we quote.

The third cause is hot tire pickup. On a 100-degree day, tires are hot enough to soften thin store-bought kits. A thick, 100% solids base coat with a hard topcoat is the fix.

Flake vs. Solid Color: Which Do You Need?

Both are full epoxy systems over the same ground slab. The difference is how they look and how they hide everyday dirt.

Full flakeSolid color
LookSpeckled, terrazzo-likeClean, single color
Hides dust and dirtVery wellShows more
TextureLight grip from the flakesSmooth unless grit is added
Hides slab flawsVery wellShows patches and cracks more
Best forDaily parking, busy family garagesShowroom look, workshops

Most homeowners pick full flake because it hides dust, tire marks and small slab flaws. Solid color suits workshops and car collectors who want a clean, bright look. Our polyaspartic garage floor coatings can use either style.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does a garage floor epoxy install take?

A typical two-car garage floor epoxy install takes one to two days, including grinding, repairs, the flake coat and the topcoat. Garages with an old coating to strip or a lot of crack repair can take an extra day.

Is garage floor epoxy slippery when wet?

A full flake garage floor has a light texture from the chips, so it grips better than smooth concrete with a sealer. For extra grip, a fine anti-slip grit can be mixed into the topcoat near the door and steps.

Do I need to empty my garage?

Yes, the floor has to be completely clear before grinding starts. Most homeowners move items to the driveway or a side yard for the one to two days of the job.

Can garage floor epoxy handle a floor jack and jack stands?

A properly installed garage floor epoxy system handles jack stands and rolling tool chests without trouble. Placing a piece of plywood under jack stands for heavy work spreads the load and protects the topcoat.
