What Does a Commercial Epoxy Install Involve?

A commercial epoxy install starts with a walk-through to map traffic, chemicals, drains and any areas that cannot shut down. From there we set a phased schedule, often nights or weekends.

The concrete is prepared with ride-on or walk-behind diamond grinders or a shot blaster, then cracks are repaired and control joints are filled with a semi-rigid filler that supports hard forklift wheels.

High-build epoxy coats go down next, followed by a topcoat matched to the use: chemical resistant for shops, anti-slip for wet areas, or urethane cement for kitchens that see hot washdowns. Safety lanes and markings finish the job.

When Do You Need Commercial Epoxy Flooring?

Dusting concrete

Forklift traffic grinds bare concrete into fine dust that settles on product and racking.

Broken joint edges

Wheels hitting open control joints chip the edges, which grows into a rough, jarring path for equipment.

Faded or missing lanes

Worn walkway and forklift lane markings are a safety problem and an inspection issue.

Oil and chemical spills

Auto shops and maintenance bays need a floor that chemicals cannot soak into.

Food and health code

Kitchens and food processing areas need a seamless floor that drains and cleans fully.

New tenant or lease turnover

A fresh coating is a fast way to bring a building up for a new tenant or buyer.

Why Do Commercial Floors Fail Faster Than Garage Floors?

Commercial floors fail faster because they take far more weight and abuse than a home garage. Hard polyurethane forklift wheels, pallet jacks and dragged pallets put heavy point loads on the coating thousands of times a week.

Open control joints are the most common weak spot. Without a proper filler, the joint edges chip and the coating breaks along the line. Chemical spills, hot washdowns and standing water add their own stress.

The answer is matching the system to the real use: a thicker build, filled joints, the right topcoat for chemicals or heat, and an inspection plan for high-traffic aisles.

What Affects the Cost of Commercial Epoxy Flooring in Fontana?

The cost of commercial epoxy flooring depends on square footage, the condition of the concrete, the coating system and the schedule. A large open warehouse floor and a small kitchen with drains and cove base are very different jobs.

Joint filling, crack repair, moisture mitigation, line striping and night or weekend work all affect the final number. Floors exposed to chemicals or thermal shock need a heavier system than dry storage areas.

Epoxy vs. Polished Concrete: Which Do You Need?

Both are common in warehouses and retail. The right one depends on spills, cleaning and the look you want.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can you coat a warehouse floor without shutting down?

Yes, most warehouse floor coatings are done in phases. We section off areas and work nights or weekends so the rest of the building keeps running, and fast-curing products can return an area to service quickly.

How long does a commercial epoxy floor last?

A commercial epoxy floor often lasts many years when the system is matched to the traffic and joints are filled correctly. High-traffic aisles may need a fresh topcoat sooner than low-traffic areas.

Can you add safety lanes and markings?

Yes, forklift lanes, walkways, hazard zones and parking stripes can be built into the coating in any standard safety color. Markings applied as part of the system last longer than paint applied over bare concrete.

What floor coating is best for a commercial kitchen?

Urethane cement is usually the best floor coating for a commercial kitchen because it handles hot water, grease and temperature swings. It can be finished with an anti-slip texture and a cove base for easy cleaning.
