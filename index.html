What Is Epoxy Flooring in Fontana?

Epoxy flooring is a two-part resin coating that bonds to bare concrete and cures into a hard, glossy surface. It is many times thicker than the garage floor paint sold in a can, and it seals the slab so oil, dust and stains stay on top where they wipe off.

Most of our work is in home garages across neighborhoods like Sierra Lakes, Hunter’s Ridge, Summit Heights and Southridge. We also coat home gyms, laundry rooms and the shop and warehouse floors that line the I-10 and I-15 corridors.

A good floor has three layers: a ground and repaired slab, a base coat that grips the concrete, and a topcoat that takes the wear. Skipping the first layer is the reason most garage coatings peel.

When Do You Need Epoxy Flooring?

These are the situations that bring most homeowners to a floor coating.

The old coating is peeling

Patches lifting near the garage door usually mean the slab was acid-etched or just washed before it was coated. The coating sat on top of the concrete instead of gripping it, and it lets go a little more every summer.

Tires are pulling up the finish

Dull rings where the car parks are hot tire pickup. Tires that just came off the freeway on a 100-degree day soften thin, low-solids coatings and peel them up when the car backs out.

Oil stains will not come out

Bare concrete soaks up oil, transmission fluid and rust like a sponge. Once the stain is deep in the slab, degreaser stops working. A sealed floor keeps the next drip on the surface.

The garage is full of dust

Bare concrete sheds a fine powder, and dry Santa Ana winds push more grit under the door. A coated floor stops the slab itself from dusting and sweeps clean in minutes.

You are turning it into a gym or shop

A home gym, workshop or game room starts with the floor. A coating handles dropped weights, rolling tool chests and spilled drinks far better than concrete or rubber mats alone.

You are getting ready to sell

A clean, bright garage floor makes the whole house show better when buyers walk through it. It is one of the quickest upgrades to finish before the house goes on the market.

How an Epoxy Floor Install Works, Step by Step

A standard two-car garage takes one to two days on site, depending on the coating system and how much repair the slab needs.

Inspect & test

We look at cracks, old coatings and drainage, and test older slabs for moisture coming up from the ground.

Diamond grind

A walk-behind grinder with a vacuum opens the surface so the coating can soak in and grip, with very little dust.

Repair

Cracks, pits and spalled edges are filled and ground flush so they do not show through the finished floor.

Base & flake

The base coat goes down and flakes are broadcast into it while wet, or the color coat is laid for a solid floor.

Topcoat & cure

A clear, UV-stable topcoat seals everything in. We tell you exactly when it is safe to walk and when to park.

Summer scheduling: resin cures fast in heat, so on hot days we start early and keep the garage door mostly closed. That keeps the coating workable and keeps wind-blown dust out of the wet finish.

How Much Does Epoxy Flooring Cost?

The cost of an epoxy floor comes down to three things: the size of the space, the condition of the concrete and the coating system you pick. A clean, newer slab needs less work than one with deep oil stains, wide cracks or a failed coating that has to be ground off.

The system matters too. A full flake floor, a solid color, a metallic design and a one-day polyaspartic floor each use different materials and labor. Commercial floors add factors like forklift traffic, joint repair and after-hours scheduling.

Is an Epoxy Floor Right for You?

An epoxy floor coating is a strong fit for most garages, but not for every slab or every plan.

A great fit if you…

  • Park in the garage and want oil and tire marks to wipe off
  • Use the space as a gym, shop or extra room
  • Want a floor that hides dust and sweeps clean
  • Plan to stay in the house for a few years or sell soon
  • Run a shop or warehouse floor that needs to stay clean and marked

Maybe not if you…

  • Have a slab with active water coming up through it
  • Have a new slab poured less than about 28 days ago
  • Need a floor that stays soft or warm underfoot
  • Plan to cover the whole floor with carpet or mats anyway

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does an epoxy garage floor last in Fontana?

A garage floor that was diamond-ground and coated with a full system usually lasts 10 years or more under normal family use. The things that shorten its life are poor prep, moisture coming up through the slab and a topcoat that is not rated for sun. Most coatings that fail early were rolled over a slab that was never ground.

How long before I can park in the garage again?

You can walk on most systems the next day. Cars should wait until the coating has cured hard, which is usually 24 to 48 hours for a polyaspartic topcoat and about 5 to 7 days for a full epoxy system. Hot weather speeds curing up, so summer installs reach drive-on strength faster than winter ones.

Will the heat make an epoxy floor peel?

Heat alone does not make a properly bonded floor peel. The real summer problem is hot tire pickup, where tires coming off the freeway soften a thin coating and lift it. A 100% solids base coat under a polyaspartic or urethane topcoat is built to resist that, and it should be part of any quote for a garage that sees daily driving.

Does epoxy turn yellow in the sun?

Standard epoxy does slowly amber when UV light hits it, which matters in garages that sit with the door open on bright afternoons. A UV-stable topcoat such as polyaspartic stops the yellowing and keeps the color true. Indoor floors that never see direct sun are much less affected.

Can you coat over cracks and old paint?

Yes, as long as the old material comes off first. Peeling paint and failed coatings are ground away, and cracks are opened and filled with a flexible repair material before the base coat goes down. Wide or uneven cracks get looked at during the estimate, because they can point to slab movement that a coating will not stop.
